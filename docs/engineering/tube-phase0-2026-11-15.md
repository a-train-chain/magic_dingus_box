# Tube CRT engine — Phase 0 timings on a Pi 5 (2026-11-15)

The design (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-tube-crt-engine-design.md`,
"Build order" step 0) asked for the shader to be timed on real hardware
before the renderer was planned. This is that measurement. **It changes the
design: approach A is out, approach B alone is not enough, and the heavier
looks need a different pipeline.**

## Method

- **Box:** the owner's `magicpi5`, Raspberry Pi 5 2 GB, V3D 7.1.10.2, Mesa
  25.0.7 (OpenGL ES 3.1), Modern TV at 1920×1080@60, Media Browser stack
  running (six containers), `v3d_freq` as shipped. 59–63 °C, never throttled.
- **Picture:** the 4:3 rectangle, 1440×1080. A playlist video was playing
  throughout (software H.264 decode), channel-guide menu style, cabinet art on.
- **Code:** a throwaway port of Sound Factory's `crt.metal` to GLSL ES on
  branch `spike/tube-phase0` (never merged), run as a transient unit in place
  of the stopped kiosk, with the production unit's scheduling and memory
  posture and `MDB_REDRAW_GATE=0`. Uniforms came from the real model
  (`tube::tube_uniforms_for` → `tube::tube_frame`).
- **Bench:** `glFinish(); t0; draw the passes offscreen; glFinish(); t1`, 31
  runs after 2 warm-ups, median reported. "Prepasses" are the quarter picture,
  the glow blur and the line raster. Afterglow adds a history write and a copy.
- **Pacing:** the interval between page-flip completions over 10 s windows,
  with the video playing. "Late" is an interval over 25 ms (a missed vblank).
- Raw data: `measurements/2026-11-15-tube-phase0-bench.log` (five bench runs)
  and `measurements/2026-11-15-tube-phase0-journal.txt`.
- **Not measured:** a Pi 4B; 720p output; the UI alone without video; heat in
  a long soak; memory traffic; anything visual beyond one screenshot (the
  picture was correct: bent glass, scanlines and mask inside the cabinet art).

## Results

### 1. One shader with uniform tests (approach A)

Milliseconds of GPU per frame at 1440×1080. The budget for 60 fps is 16.7 ms
for everything, the video and UI included.

| Drawn | exact sRGB | square/sqrt gamma |
|---|---:|---:|
| plain copy (the floor) | 1.7 | 1.9 |
| **tube, every row neutral or off** | **20.2** | **18.6** |
| monitor | 27.3 | 24.3 |
| pvm | 29.4 | 26.3 |
| trinitron | 52.2 | 44.2 |
| tv | 60.6 | 52.6 |
| arcade | 60.4 | 52.3 |
| everything | 60.7 | 52.6 |
| everything, full 1920×1080 | 81.6 | 71.1 |

**A switched-off effect is not free.** With every effect off the shader still
costs ten times a copy. On screen, PVM ran at 20 fps (30 with the cheap gamma).
The driver's own statistics say why: the full shader is 1302 instructions and
drops the GPU to 2-thread mode.

What each effect adds to `everything` (exact sRGB): true raster 15.5 ms,
convergence 10.2, sharpness 9.4, chroma bleed 5.2, mask 4.6, glow 3.8, noise
1.4, curvature 1.4, afterglow about 1. Scanline depth, jitter, hum, flicker,
the glass rows and the wear rows: about 0. Cost scales with pixels (1.33× the
pixels, 1.34× the time). The exact sRGB curve costs 1.6–8 ms more than
square/sqrt, depending on how many taps a look takes.

### 2. Compiling only what a look uses (approach B)

Same shader with each effect behind a compile-time switch, cheap gamma.

| Variant compiled | Instructions | Threads | First-draw hitch | GPU ms, its own look |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| nothing (one sample × brightness) | 42 | 4 | 6–12 ms | 2.6 |
| pvm's effects | 290 | 4 | 18 ms | 12.5 (monitor) · 13.0 (pvm, no afterglow) · 15.6 (pvm) |
| tv's effects | 983 | 2 | 285 ms | 43.4 |
| everything, runtime switches | 1302 | 2 | 465–645 ms | 52.6 |

Compiling out works: the empty variant is near the floor. But a compiled
variant still pays for its own effects whether their rows are on or off (the
pvm variant with every row neutral: 9.2 ms; the tv variant: 15.9 ms), and the
tv-class looks stay far over budget.

### 3. What the screen can afford, video playing

| Shader and look | GPU ms (bench) | On screen |
|---|---:|---|
| nothing compiled + monitor's glow prepass | 5.1 | 60 fps, 0.3–0.7% late |
| pvm variant, monitor look | 12.5 | 52–60 fps, 3–16% late |
| pvm variant, pvm look | 15.6 | locked to 30 fps |

**The tube has about 8 ms of GPU a frame, prepasses included, if a look is to
hold 60 fps with a video playing.** 12.5 ms already misses the 99% target in
the design's Acceptance section.

### 4. Other facts

- Shader build: compile 15–18 ms and link 60–110 ms for the big program, but
  the real hitch is the first draw (table above). All seven programs
  rebuilt in 226 ms.
- The driver has `GL_EXT_color_buffer_float`, `GL_EXT_color_buffer_half_float`,
  `GL_EXT_sRGB_write_control`, `GL_EXT_texture_sRGB_decode` and
  `GL_EXT_disjoint_timer_query`.
- Kiosk CPU stayed at 8–17% of one core: the limit is the GPU, not the CPU or
  RAM (about 1 GB available throughout).

## What this means for the design

1. **Approach A is out** on this GPU. §3's "each effect is skipped by a
   uniform test" does not hold.
2. **Approach B is necessary and not sufficient.** The lightest shipped look
   compiles to 12.5–15.6 ms against a budget near 8.
3. **The tv-class looks cannot be one full-resolution pass.** Raster,
   convergence, sharpness and chroma bleed are per-pixel multi-tap work; done
   per output pixel in one large shader they cost 43 ms.
4. **Program size is itself a cost.** Past a few hundred instructions the GPU
   halves its thread count, and the first-draw hitch grows to hundreds of ms.
   Several small passes beat one large one.

## What to try next (a second timing round, before the renderer is planned)

Aimed at the 8 ms budget, in the order they are expected to pay:

1. **A signal pass at signal resolution.** Tone, sharpness, chroma bleed and
   convergence computed once into a texture the size of the raster
   (picture width × lines, or the video's own size), so the output pass reads
   one or two taps per pixel instead of nine to thirty.
2. **Hardware sRGB decode.** With `GL_EXT_sRGB_write_control` the scene can be
   an sRGB texture whose writes are left unencoded, so sampling decodes and
   filters in linear light for free and every decode `pow` goes away.
3. **Bake what depends only on position** (opening, rim, vignette, glass
   black, reflection) into one texture rebuilt when a setting changes, and the
   mask into a small repeating texture.
4. **A cheaper beam profile** (per-channel `sqrt`, `exp` and divide today).
5. **The curvature warp in the vertex stage** on a coarse grid.
6. If a look still cannot reach 8 ms: draw the tube at 30 fps while a video
   plays (playlist video is 30 fps or less), which roughly triples the budget,
   or ship that look with the costly rows lowered, as the design's Acceptance
   section already allows.

# Round 2 (same day): a restructured pipeline

The list above was built as "pipeline 2" in the same spike and timed in a
second window on the same box, same picture, a playlist video playing. Raw
log: `measurements/2026-11-15-tube-phase0-round2-journal.txt`.

Pipeline 2: hardware sRGB decode of the scene (`GL_EXT_sRGB_write_control`),
a signal pass (tone, sharpness, chroma bleed, convergence, the horizontal
displacements) into a half-float texture, a line pass reading it, a small
output pass (bend, beam, mask from a texture, baked glass, glow, noise, hum),
and the afterglow history. Each program compiles only the effects its look
uses. Per-pass GPU time is from `GL_TIME_ELAPSED` queries.

## Results

### The six looks (GPU ms per frame, 1440×1080)

| Look | Round 1, one shader | Round 1, compiled per look | **Pipeline 2** | glow | signal | lines | output | history |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| monitor | 24.3–27.3 | 12.5 | **14.1** | 2.6 | 4.4 | – | 7.0 | – |
| pvm | 26.3–29.4 | 15.6 | **17.1** | 3.4 | 4.4 | – | 7.0 | 2.1 |
| trinitron | 44.2–52.2 | – | **26.6** | 4.1 | 7.1 | 2.0 | 11.0 | 2.1 |
| tv | 52.6–60.6 | 43.4 | **30.5** | 4.7 | 10.0 | 2.0 | 11.5 | 2.1 |
| arcade | 52.3–60.4 | – | **29.6** | 5.2 | 10.0 | 2.0 | 10.1 | 2.1 |
| everything | 52.6–60.7 | – | **30.5** | 4.7 | 10.0 | 2.0 | 11.5 | 2.1 |

The heavy looks got much cheaper (tv 43 → 30). The light looks did not: every
extra full-picture pass costs 2–4 ms on this GPU whatever it computes (a plain
copy is 1.8 ms), so splitting pvm's work into more passes cost more than it
saved.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| pvm | 2.9 | 4.4 | – | 7.0 | 4.5 | 18.8 | 30 fps |

| pvm, cheap beam | 2.9 | 4.4 | – | 6.3 | 4.4 | 18.1 | 30 fps |

| pvm, no afterglow | 2.9 | 4.4 | – | 9.1 | – | 16.4 | 30 fps |
| pvm, no afterglow or glow, half width, cheap beam, mesh, 8-bit | – | 2.1 | – | 8.2 | – | 10.4 | 30 fps |
| pvm, no afterglow or glow, no signal work | – | 3.1 | – | 8.9 | – | 11.9 | 30 fps |
| monitor | 2.3 | 4.4 | – | 9.0 | – | 15.7 | 30 fps |
| tv | 4.2 | 10.1 | 2.1 | 11.6 | 4.3 | 32.2 | 20 fps |
| tv, half width + cheap beam | 4.3 | 5.6 | 1.2 | 9.9 | 4.7 | 25.7 | 20 fps |
| tv, half width + cheap beam, no afterglow or glow | 0.7 | 5.1 | 1.0 | 11.2 | – | 18.0 | 30 fps |

What each option is worth: signal at half width −2.2 ms (pvm) to −4.5 ms (tv);
cheap beam −0.7 to −1.7 ms; the vertex-stage bend nothing; the 8-bit signal
texture −0.3 ms; the mask texture and baked glass are in every row. The glow
chain alone is about 3 ms and afterglow 2–4.5 ms. A signal pass with nothing to
do still costs 3 ms and must be skipped outright.

**Caveat on the "on screen" column:** in round 2 the spike read its GPU timers
back every frame, which may itself have cost frame pacing; round 1 had no such
read-back and held 60 fps at 5 ms and a rock-steady 30 fps (33.33 ms, max
33.42) at 15.6 ms. The per-pass numbers are sound; the 30-versus-60 boundary
is still "about 8 ms" from round 1, not re-measured here.

### Program sizes, and a compiler hang

- Pipeline 2's programs are 130–282 instructions and mostly stay in 4-thread
  mode (two output variants at 268 and 281 instructions dropped to 2 threads).
  First draw is 34 ms at worst, against 285–645 ms for the round 1 shaders.
- **Mesa's shader compiler hung** while the bench was building one variant
  (the "everything, one row changed" set, right after the six looks): the
  render thread sat inside `libgallium` under the program build at 100% of a
  core for more than nine minutes, until the process was killed. Which define
  set triggered it was not isolated. On a customer's box that is a frozen TV
  until the watchdog restarts the kiosk, and then again on the next start.

## What round 2 settles

1. **No arrangement tried reaches 8 ms for a look with glow or afterglow at
   1440×1080.** One output pass with the bend, scanlines, mask and roll-off is
   7–9 ms by itself. The Pi 5 can hold 60 fps with a video only for a look
   that is essentially that one pass and nothing else.
2. **The looks as designed are 30 fps looks on this box.** monitor and pvm cost
   14–19 ms and held an even 30 fps; a video is 30 fps or less anyway, but
   menu movement would be 30 fps too.
3. **The tv-class looks need lowering even for 30 fps.** As shipped they are
   30 ms (20 fps). At half signal width with the cheap beam and without glow
   and afterglow they are 18 ms (30 fps).
4. **Shader variants must be a fixed, pre-tested set**, never compiled on
   demand from whatever combination of rows is non-neutral: one combination
   hung the driver's compiler.
5. **Not tried:** 720p output (0.44× the pixels, so roughly 2.25× cheaper:
   pvm would be near 7–8 ms); a compiled single-pass variant combined with
   hardware decode and the mask texture (round 1's 15.6 ms pvm had neither);
   a cheaper glow chain; the Enhanced engine's own cost for comparison.

## The decision this leaves for the owner

The renderer plan cannot be written until one of these is chosen:

- **A. Tube draws at 30 fps.** Keeps glow, afterglow and the look of the
  lighter looks as designed; the tv-class looks ship with glow/afterglow
  lowered or off. Videos are unaffected (30 fps or less); menus and the guide
  move at 30 fps while Tube is on.
- **B. Tube stays at 60 fps and is lean.** One pass: bend, scanlines, mask,
  roll-off, tone, vignette, the supply and wear effects that cost nothing.
  No glow, afterglow, sharpness, convergence, chroma bleed or true raster.
  Closer to today's Enhanced engine with better masks and glass.
- **C. Both, chosen per look.** Light looks at 60 fps, rich looks at 30 fps,
  with the row table saying which rows force 30 fps.
- **D. Tube at 720p output.** Probably 60 fps for the lighter looks as
  designed; costs picture sharpness on the menus and 1080p video, and has not
  been measured.
